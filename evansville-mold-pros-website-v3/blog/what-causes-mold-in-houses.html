What Causes Mold in a House? 7 Common Reasons

Mold can develop in a home when moisture remains on surfaces or inside building materials. A leaking pipe, damp basement, roof problem, or humid bathroom can create conditions that allow mold to grow.

Evansville Mold ProsOctober 20267 min read

Finding the cause matters because cleaning visible mold alone may not resolve the problem. If the affected area remains damp, mold may return.

For homeowners in Evansville, Indiana, understanding common sources of indoor moisture can help identify problems early and determine when further assessment may be needed.

What Does Mold Need to Grow?

Mold reproduces through tiny spores that can be present in indoor and outdoor air. These spores can grow when they land on suitable materials and encounter enough moisture.

Common materials that may support mold growth include drywall, wood, paper products, ceiling tiles, fabrics, and some types of insulation.

Moisture is a key factor. It can come from liquid water entering a building, humidity condensing on cool surfaces, or materials that remain wet after a leak.

Controlling moisture and drying affected materials are important steps in reducing the conditions that allow mold to develop.

1. Plumbing Leaks

Leaking pipes and plumbing fixtures are common sources of indoor moisture. A slow leak may continue for weeks before it becomes obvious.

Check beneath sinks, around toilets, near washing machines, and around visible plumbing connections. Water may also escape behind cabinets or inside walls, where the damage is harder to notice.

Possible warning signs include:

  • Swollen cabinets or baseboards
  • Water stains near pipes
  • Damp flooring
  • Peeling paint
  • Persistent musty odors

If you find a leak, arrange repairs and dry affected materials when safe. If water has reached drywall, insulation, or other concealed materials, further assessment may be necessary.

2. Roof Leaks and Damaged Roofing

Water entering through a damaged roof can affect attic insulation, roof sheathing, ceilings, and nearby walls.

Leaks may occur around damaged shingles, flashing, roof penetrations, or other vulnerable areas. Sometimes, the first visible sign is a ceiling stain rather than water dripping directly into a room.

After heavy rain, pay attention to new stains, damp attic materials, or recurring discoloration. Do not enter an unsafe attic or climb onto a roof to investigate.

Roof repairs and mold remediation may be separate tasks. Correcting the source of water is important before affected materials can be restored properly.

Learn more about Attic Mold Remediation and Removal.

3. High Indoor Humidity

Indoor humidity can contribute to mold growth, particularly when moisture accumulates on walls, windows, pipes, or other cool surfaces.

Bathrooms, kitchens, laundry rooms, and poorly ventilated spaces can produce or retain moisture from everyday activities.

To help manage humidity:

  1. Use exhaust fans that vent outdoors where appropriate.
  2. Monitor indoor humidity with a hygrometer.
  3. Use a dehumidifier when needed.
  4. Dry condensation from affected surfaces.
  5. Investigate recurring dampness rather than repeatedly wiping the same area.

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 60 percent when possible, ideally between 30 and 50 percent.

A dehumidifier can reduce moisture in the air, but it will not fix a leaking pipe, roof leak, or water intrusion problem.

4. Condensation on Cold Surfaces

Condensation occurs when moisture in the air collects on a surface that is cool enough for water vapor to condense.

It may appear on windows, cold water pipes, exterior walls, or other surfaces. If the moisture repeatedly collects and materials do not dry, conditions may become suitable for mold growth.

Condensation can be influenced by indoor humidity, surface temperature, insulation, and air movement.

If you regularly see water droplets on windows or damp patches on walls, consider whether humidity is high or whether the surface is unusually cold. Persistent condensation may need a more detailed assessment to identify the contributing conditions.

5. Basement Moisture and Water Intrusion

Basements may experience moisture from plumbing leaks, water entering through foundation walls, drainage problems, or condensation.

Signs of a possible moisture issue include damp walls, water stains, musty odors, peeling coatings, and wet belongings stored near affected areas.

Check whether gutters and downspouts are clear and whether rainwater is directed away from the foundation where appropriate. Persistent water entry may require drainage or foundation repairs.

Do not assume every basement moisture problem can be solved with a dehumidifier alone. The correct response depends on where the water comes from and how it reaches the building.

Read more about Basement Mold Remediation.

6. Damp Crawl Spaces

Crawl spaces can develop moisture problems involving the ground, plumbing, outside water entry, or condensation. Limited access can make it difficult to spot these conditions early.

Excess moisture may affect wooden framing, insulation, and other materials beneath the home.

Possible signs include damp insulation, standing water, visible discoloration, or a persistent musty smell coming from the area.

Investigating a crawl space can involve access and safety risks. If the area is difficult to reach or shows signs of extensive moisture damage, consider seeking appropriate professional assistance.

Learn about Crawl Space Mold Remediation.

7. Water Damage That Was Not Dried Properly

After a leak, plumbing failure, or other water intrusion event, materials may remain damp even when the visible surface looks dry.

Drywall, flooring, insulation, wood, and other porous materials can retain moisture. If they stay wet long enough, mold may develop.

After water damage:

  • Stop the water source when it is safe to do so.
  • Remove standing water where appropriate.
  • Dry wet materials promptly.
  • Check for moisture that may remain inside building materials.
  • Arrange further assessment if materials remain damp or become damaged.

The EPA advises drying water damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours when possible to help prevent mold growth. This is a useful target, not a guarantee that mold will not develop.

If water has affected concealed areas or extensive building materials, surface drying alone may not be enough.

Can Mold Grow Without a Plumbing Leak?

Yes. A plumbing leak is only one possible source of moisture.

Mold may develop because of condensation, high indoor humidity, roof leaks, water entering through the foundation, damp crawl spaces, or materials that were not fully dried after water damage.

The absence of a visible leak does not rule out a moisture problem. If you notice recurring dampness or an unexplained musty odor, investigate the conditions rather than assuming the problem is harmless.

At the same time, a musty smell or water stain alone does not prove that mold is present.

How Can You Find the Cause of Mold?

Start by observing where the problem appears and whether moisture is present.

Look for nearby plumbing, roof damage, condensation, water stains, or other evidence of water intrusion. Consider whether the issue appears after rain, after showers, or during periods of high humidity.

An inspection may help identify visible growth, damaged materials, and possible moisture sources. However, hidden areas may require additional investigation if there are signs that water has reached concealed materials.

Mold testing may be useful when it answers a specific question, but it is not automatically required whenever mold is visible. The EPA notes that sampling is often unnecessary when visible mold is present and the affected area can be addressed appropriately.

Learn more about Mold Inspection and Mold Testing.

How to Reduce the Risk of Mold Returning

Once mold has developed, the likelihood of recurrence depends in part on whether moisture conditions are corrected.

Useful prevention steps include:

  1. Repair plumbing and roof leaks promptly.
  2. Control indoor humidity.
  3. Use suitable ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens.
  4. Dry wet materials quickly after water intrusion.
  5. Investigate recurring condensation.
  6. Check basements, crawl spaces, and attics for moisture.
  7. Address damaged materials and the underlying water source.

Avoid painting over suspected mold or repeatedly cleaning the same area without investigating why it becomes damp.
