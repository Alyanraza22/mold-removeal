DIY or professional?

Can You Clean Mold Yourself?

Sometimes. A small patch on a suitable hard surface may be manageable if you can correct the moisture source and clean the area safely.

The EPA recommends the following precautions:

Fix the leak or moisture problem first.
Scrub suitable hard surfaces with detergent and water, then dry them completely.
Use appropriate protection, including gloves, goggles, and a properly fitted N95 respirator when appropriate.
Do not paint or caulk over mold before addressing the underlying problem.
Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaning products. Dangerous fumes can form.
Remember that porous materials such as carpet, ceiling tiles, and some insulation may need to be discarded if they cannot be cleaned properly.

Bleach is not recommended as a routine solution for mold cleanup. Killing mold does not automatically remove the material left behind.

Consider professional help if the affected area is larger than about 10 square feet, there has been extensive water damage, you suspect hidden mold, or the water involved came from sewage or another contaminated source.

If you suspect mold inside a heating or cooling system, avoid running it until you have received appropriate guidance. If anyone in the home has health concerns, consult a health professional.

Inspection and testing

Mold Inspection vs. Mold Testing vs. Remediation

These terms refer to different services.

01

Mold inspection

involves looking for visible growth, moisture problems, and areas where mold may be hidden.

02

Mold testing

involves collecting samples for laboratory analysis. Testing can answer certain questions, but it is not necessary for every mold problem.

03

Mold remediation

is the work performed to address the affected area, including appropriate cleaning or removal and moisture control.

The EPA says sampling is usually unnecessary when visible mold is already present. There are no federal standards establishing acceptable indoor mold or mold spore levels for ordinary residential buildings.

Before paying for testing, ask what the test is meant to establish and how the result would change the work plan. If testing is recommended, ask who will collect the samples and how the results will be interpreted.

More information is available through the EPA's mold guidance.

Good to know

Evansville Homes, Heavy Rain, and Moisture Problems

Evansville's location near the Ohio River makes flooding and water management important local concerns. However, a major flood is not the only way mold can develop. Plumbing leaks, roof damage, damp basements, and water intrusion during heavy rain can also leave building materials wet.

Pay attention to places that become damp repeatedly. If water enters the same part of your basement after rain, note where it appears and how long the area stays wet. That pattern can help a contractor investigate the source.

Crawl spaces deserve attention too. Moisture beneath a house can affect wood framing and insulation without being immediately visible from inside the living area.

After flooding or a significant leak, start drying affected materials as soon as it is safe. Water can reach wall cavities, flooring, and insulation even when the surface looks dry. Tell the contractor whether the water came from a plumbing supply line, outside flooding, or sewage because the source affects cleanup precautions.

What affects your quote?

The contractor may need to account for:

  • The size and location of the affected area
  • Whether mold is visible or hidden behind building materials

  • Removal and replacement of drywall, insulation, flooring, or other materials
  • Cleaning and drying
  • Repairs to the source of moisture

Roofing, plumbing, and other building repairs may be billed separately. Ask for a written scope that identifies what is included, what may need removal, and which repairs are excluded.

Will Homeowners Insurance Pay for Mold Removal?

Sometimes. Coverage depends on your policy and the cause of the damage.

Mold related to a sudden covered event, such as a burst pipe, may be treated differently from mold caused by long term humidity or neglected maintenance. Flood damage may fall under separate policy terms or a separate policy.

Before authorizing work, contact your insurer and ask:

Does my policy cover the event that caused the water damage?
Is mold remediation covered in this situation?
What photos, estimates, or inspections do you need?
Should an inspection take place before cleanup begins?

Keep photos and records when it is safe to do so. Only your insurer can explain how your policy applies. Be cautious of anyone who promises that insurance will cover the entire job before the claim has been reviewed.

Prevention

How to Help Keep Mold From Returning

Mold prevention starts with moisture control. Cleaning a damp surface will not solve a leak or drainage problem.

Fix plumbing and roof leaks promptly.
Keep gutters clear and in good repair.
Make sure the ground drains away from the foundation where possible.
Keep indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent when practical.
Use bathroom exhaust fans while showering and kitchen exhaust fans when cooking.
Insulate cold water pipes where condensation is a problem.
Keep air conditioner drip pans clean and drain lines clear.
Check areas that become damp after heavy rain.

The EPA recommends drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours when possible. This is a prevention guideline, not a guarantee that mold will never develop.

For renters

Renting in Evansville?

If you rent your home and find a leak, damp wall, or suspected mold, report the problem to your landlord or property manager promptly.

Put the report in writing and keep a copy, along with dated photos of the affected area. If ongoing moisture problems are not addressed, contact the appropriate local or state housing authority for guidance.

Check your lease and local requirements before arranging work yourself. Responsibility for repairs and payment can depend on the circumstances.
